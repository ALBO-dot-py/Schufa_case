02.1 · ARTICLE 22 / GDPR

A prohibition in principle.

Not simply a right to object. Three conditions must be met.

01
A decision
Even an intermediate score, if it determines the outcome.
02
Solely automated processing
Without meaningful human involvement.
03
Legal or similarly significant effects
For example, refusal of credit and its financial consequences.

02.2 · THE COURT’S RULING

A credit score can itself be a decision.

Article 22 GDPR applies when the score plays a determining role in the bank’s decision to grant or refuse credit.

SCHUFA

Credit score

Produces the assessment.

→
BANK

Credit decision

Relies heavily on the score.

SCHUFA is therefore effectively making a decision too.

02.3 · ACCOUNTABILITY / THE RISK

Who can explain the decision?

Without the Court’s interpretation, each actor could shift responsibility.

SCHUFA

“We only provide a score.”

The agency could deny responsibility for the final outcome.

THE BANK

“We don’t know how it was calculated.”

The bank could lack the information needed to explain the score.

Without this interpretation, the individual could have no one to turn to.

The BOSCO case

05 · CASE STUDY / THE FACTS

What is BOSCO?

Spanish government software used by electricity retailers to check eligibility for the bono social, a statutory discount for vulnerable consumers.

2018 · CIVIO

Access requested

The transparency organisation requested the source code to scrutinise the automated system.

MITECO · THE MINISTRY

Source code withheld

Specifications and tests were disclosed, but the code was refused on intellectual property and security grounds.

The dispute: can the public inspect the code behind a statutory right?

05 · CASE STUDY / THE RULING

The Supreme Court’s findings.

01
Code as administrative reasoning
When social rights are determined automatically, the code embodies the reasoning.
02
Security through obscurity is not enough
Independent auditing and safeguards can address security risks.
03
Public interest over blanket IP claims
Public intellectual property claims carry less weight than legal scrutiny.

The code must be open to meaningful scrutiny.

06 · RESEARCH QUESTION & RESULTS
06.1

Public
authorities

06.1 · PUBLIC AUTHORITIES

The code is the law in action.

Public administrations exercise sovereign power and distribute public funds.

01 · DEMOCRATIC ACCOUNTABILITY

Law → Code

When software translates legislation into eligibility criteria, auditing the code verifies whether the law is correctly applied.

02 · NO COMMERCIAL RIVALRY

Weaker IP claims

Public authorities do not rely on market exclusivity or profit incentives in the same way as private competitors.

Stronger transparency: access to code where needed for democratic and legal scrutiny.

06 · RESEARCH QUESTION & RESULTS
06.2

Private
companies

06.2 · PRIVATE COMPANIES

Protect innovation.
Not unaccountable power.

01 · COMPETITIVE MARKETS

Trade secrets

Compulsory publication of raw source code can undermine innovation incentives and enable copying.

02 · TAILORED TRANSPARENCY

Explain & audit

Meaningful explanations and independent auditing can preserve accountability without indiscriminate disclosure.

Commercial secrecy is legitimate. Unaccountable algorithmic power is not.

06.3 · RESULTS / RESEARCH QUESTION

Should private companies and public authorities be subject to different standards of algorithmic transparency?

Yes.Different standards.
Same accountability.

06.3 · RESULTS / ASYMMETRIC TRANSPARENCY

Why different standards?

PUBLIC AUTHORITIES

Stronger transparency

Sovereign power and statutory rights justify code-level scrutiny where needed.

≠
PRIVATE COMPANIES

Tailored transparency

Competition and trade secrets justify functional explanations and audited transparency.

COMMON FLOOR

Effective accountability and individual rights in both sectors.

06.3 · RESULTS / COMMON FLOOR OF RIGHTS

A common floor of
individual rights.

When algorithms significantly affect people’s lives, functional explainability should answer three questions.

01 · INPUTS

Which data?

What personal data was used?

02 · LOGIC

How weighted?

How did that data influence the decision?

03 · OUTCOME

Why this result?

Why was this particular decision made?

Trade secrecy cannot eliminate the right to understand a consequential decision.

06.3 · CONCLUSION

Asymmetric transparency.
Accountability for all.
